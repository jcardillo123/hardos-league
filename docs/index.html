xW is expected wins from the all-play record — what each week's score would have earned against the whole league instead of one scheduled opponent. Luck is actual wins minus that. Eff% is points scored as a share of the best lineup the roster could have fielded, which unlike luck is the manager's own doing. Regular season only.

Click either side of a matchup to see the roster that was set, with the bench underneath. Opt is the best lineup that roster could have produced.

Schedule luck

Positive means the schedule handed over wins the scores did not earn. All-play rate is computed per week before it is summed, so the 10-team and 12-team seasons are comparable.

Points left on the bench

The weeks with the largest gap between what was started and what was available. COST A WIN marks the ones where the optimal lineup would have flipped the result. Players on IR are excluded — ESPN would not have let them start.

Row manager's record against each column. Click a cell for the full game log. Managers are matched on ESPN owner id, so a manager who renamed their team still lines up across seasons.

All seasons folded together. Click a row for the season-by-season line.

Trades, graded

One row per side of a trade, best return first — so the winner of a deal and the manager who lost it are the same trade seen twice. Net is what the players received went on to score from that week onwards, minus what the players sent away scored for their new team, counted only while each one stayed on the roster he moved to. Before is what the two sides had already scored that season when the deal was struck, which is what the managers were looking at: a side that gave up a big number before and a small one after sold at the right time. Grades are z-scores of net across all 65 trades in league history, so the scope control never changes a letter. Click a row for every player in the deal.

Best waiver and free-agent pickups

Ranked by started points after the pickup rather than total points, because a pickup is only worth what actually made it into the lineup. Points stop counting the week the player left that roster.

Who deals, and how it goes

Every trade side and every pickup folded together per manager. Trade net sums to zero across the league by construction, so this is a ranking of the same pie being divided.

Trades are reconstructed by diffing week-to-week rosters, because ESPN serves no player list for 55 of the 65 trades on record. Players dropped and claimed by somebody else in the same week are excluded first so they cannot be mistaken for a trade. A ONE SIDED flag means only one direction of a deal was recoverable — either a leg of a three-team trade, or a player cut the moment he arrived — so the grade on that row is missing whatever came back the other way.
